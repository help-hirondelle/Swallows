Solutions Catalog

Existing solutions can help, but isolated one-off measures often underperform. This page summarizes what each measure addresses and one practical limitation to plan for.

Solution Matrix

Solution What problem it solves Where it is useful Limitation to plan for
Artificial nests on facades Adds nesting opportunities where natural attachment points were lost Renovated facades and new projects, especially near existing colonies Works only under suitable technical conditions and placement choices
Droppings boards / shelves Reduces soiling under nests and lowers complaint pressure Entrances, balconies, walkways, and high-traffic paths Hard to maintain at height; neglected boards can accumulate waste, attract pigeons, and increase risk
Mud trays or controlled mud zones Improves access to nest-building material where soil is sealed Highly impermeable urban areas with little exposed moist soil Sometimes ineffective depending on location and maintenance
Green and insect-supporting habitat Supports food availability for insectivorous birds Neighborhood and district-scale planning Benefits are slower and require consistent habitat management
Colony mapping and monitoring Targets interventions where decline/conflict is strongest Municipal and cantonal coordination Needs repeated data collection and shared governance to stay useful
Coordinated municipal action Aligns permits, funding, communication, and implementation timing Multi-site programs and inter-communal coordination Fragmented implementation across departments reduces impact

Implementation Checklists

Site-Level Package

  • Pair nest support with droppings management and a maintenance schedule.
  • Check roof overhang and facade roughness before selecting artificial nest locations.
  • Track complaints and occupancy after implementation to adjust quickly.

Program-Level Package

  • Coordinate owners, builders, and municipal teams before renovation starts.
  • Use mapping and monitoring to prioritize locations and avoid random deployment.
  • Scale only measures that show occupancy and reduced conflict in pilot sites.

Selection Rule

Combine measures, do not isolate them. In this project context, coexistence improves when technical nesting support, nuisance management, monitoring, and municipal coordination are implemented together.
