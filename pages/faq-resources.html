FAQ and Resources

Practical questions from owners, builders, and municipalities, answered using project report findings. Missing official links and project assets are marked explicitly.

FAQ

Why are swallows declining in Lausanne and Vaud?

The report links decline to urban conditions: smoother renovated facades, fewer overhangs, less access to mud, reduced insect availability, and management practices that remove nesting opportunities.

Why do they nest on buildings?

House martins depend almost entirely on human-made structures for nesting. Building design therefore has direct impact on colony survival.

Why are droppings such a big issue?

Interviews identified droppings under nests as a major conflict driver. When hygiene and aesthetic concerns are not managed, tolerance declines and nests are more likely to be blocked or removed.

Do artificial nests always work?

No. They can help, but performance depends on technical conditions such as placement, facade characteristics, and proximity to existing colonies.

Why not just move or block nests?

Blocking or removing nests may reduce conflict at one point but worsens the overall decline and shifts conflict elsewhere. The project approach is coexistence: protect nesting and reduce nuisance together.

Why should municipalities care?

Municipalities can coordinate across sites, reduce complaints and cleaning burden, improve biodiversity outcomes, and demonstrate visible public action through well-managed programs.
