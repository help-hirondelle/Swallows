Guidance for Constructors and Architects

Contemporary renovation and architectural choices can unintentionally prevent nesting: smooth surfaces, loss of overhangs, and anti-nesting details reduce viable nest points. The report supports integrating biodiversity early, instead of trying to patch conflicts at the end.

Design Integration Points

Facade Roughness and Attachment

If surfaces are too smooth, nests do not attach reliably. Preserve or create enough roughness and sheltered attachment areas where nest solutions are planned.

Shelter and Roof Geometry

Keep or recreate protected placement under roof edges. As a practical reference, overhangs should be around 30 cm or more where nesting support is intended.

Access and Maintenance

Include safe access for cleaning and inspection. Droppings boards and artificial nests fail faster when maintenance access is missing.

Project Workflow Recommendations

Phase Recommended Step Output
Pre-design Map existing nests/colonies and identify likely conflict points below facades Baseline coexistence constraints
Concept and permit package Integrate nest support, droppings mitigation, and maintenance access in drawings Buildable package with fewer late redesigns
Detailed design Place artificial nests in suitable locations, preferably near existing colonies when possible Technically coherent nesting layout
Execution and handover Plan works and handover rules to avoid predictable conflict when birds attempt nesting Reduced reactive blocking and complaint risk

Delivery Tip

Avoid one-off fixes. Artificial nests, facade details, and droppings management perform better as one coordinated package, with predator risk and nearby vegetation considered in final placement.
