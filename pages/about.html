About This Project

Help Hirondelle turns swallow conservation into practical coexistence guidance for residents, building owners, schools, and communes in Lausanne and Vaud.

Swallows in Our Cities

Swallows are emblematic birds of towns, villages, and rural buildings. Their return is associated with spring, but their presence is becoming less secure because buildings, open soil, insects, and human tolerance are changing.

Why Protection Matters

They depend on our buildings

House martins usually nest under roof overhangs on facades. Barn swallows prefer barns, stables, hangars, garages, and other closed or semi-open spaces.

They indicate biodiversity health

Swallows feed almost entirely on flying insects. Their decline points to wider losses of insects, green areas, and ecological quality in urban and rural ecosystems.

They provide natural insect control

One pair of barn swallows may need around 150,000 flies, about 1 kg of food, to raise a brood.

They return to known sites

Swallows often return to former nesting places. If a site disappears after renovation or demolition, relocation can be difficult.

Main Threats

Lost nesting sites

Renovations, demolitions, sealed openings, and smooth facades remove usable nest places.

Lack of mud

Paved and impermeable surfaces reduce access to the clay-rich mud needed for nest building.

Fewer insects

Intensive agriculture, pesticides, light pollution, and loss of green areas reduce food.

Breeding-season disturbance

Construction work can disrupt birds when they are nesting or feeding young.

Droppings conflict

Low tolerance of soiling can lead people to remove, block, or discourage nests.

Late measures

Conservation works best when planned early, not added after a colony is already at risk.

Legal Protection

Swallows and their nests are legally protected. Before renovation, demolition, or maintenance work, nesting sites should be checked and protected so birds, eggs, nests, and breeding sites are not harmed or removed.
