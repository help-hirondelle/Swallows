The Project

Help Hirondelle translates swallow conservation into practical coexistence guidance for residents, building owners, schools, construction teams, and communes in Lausanne and Vaud.

Swallows in our cities

Swallows are emblematic birds of towns, villages, and rural buildings. They are often associated with the return of spring, but their presence is becoming less secure. In Switzerland and the Canton of Vaud, both the house martin and the barn swallow are priority species for conservation.

Their decline is not caused by one single factor. It is linked to fewer suitable nesting sites, lack of mud, fewer insects, building renovation, urban densification, and lower tolerance from residents when nests create mess.

Why swallows need protection

They depend on human buildings

House martins usually nest under roof overhangs on facades. Barn swallows prefer barns, stables, hangars, garages, laundry rooms, covered passages, and other closed or semi-open spaces. Their survival is closely connected to how we design, renovate, and maintain buildings.

They reveal ecological change

Swallows feed almost entirely on flying insects. Their decline is a warning sign of broader biodiversity loss caused by fewer green areas, light pollution, intensive agriculture, pesticides, and simplified urban landscapes.

They help regulate insects

By feeding on flies, midges, and other small insects, swallows help maintain a healthier urban environment. One pair of barn swallows may need around 150,000 flies, about 1 kg of food, to raise a brood.

They are loyal to nest sites

Swallows often return each spring to former nesting places. If a traditional site disappears after renovation, demolition, or facade modification, the birds may struggle to relocate successfully.

How urban change affects them

Smoother facades

Modern surfaces often lack the roughness needed for mud nests to adhere properly.

Fewer overhangs and openings

Traditional roof features, barn openings, and sheltered spaces are often removed or sealed.

Less mud

Paved roads, asphalt, and sealed surfaces make natural mud rare exactly where birds need it.

Less tolerance

Droppings under nests can trigger complaints and lead people to remove, block, or discourage nests.

Main threats

Loss of nesting sites

Renovations, demolitions, sealed openings, and smooth facades remove usable nest places.

Lack of mud

Paved and impermeable surfaces reduce access to the clay-rich mud needed for nest building.

Fewer flying insects

Intensive agriculture, pesticides, light pollution, and loss of green areas reduce food.

Breeding-season disturbance

Construction work can disrupt birds when they are nesting or feeding young.

Droppings conflict

Low tolerance of soiling can lead residents or owners to remove, block, or discourage nests.

Late or fragmented measures

Conservation works best when planned early, not added after a colony is already at risk.

Legal protection

Swallows and their nests are legally protected. In Switzerland, protected birds must not be killed, injured, captured, or disturbed through the destruction or removal of eggs, nests, or breeding sites. Before renovation, demolition, or maintenance work, nesting sites should be checked and preserved.

Protection is also about keeping a site capable of reproduction. A project that removes nests, blocks access, or changes a building so swallows can no longer use it can seriously affect a colony.
