About House Martins

This project addresses decline in Lausanne and the canton of Vaud. House martins depend almost entirely on human-made structures for nesting, so changes in facades, roofs, and urban management directly affect whether colonies can persist.

Why This Matters in Cities

Urban Ecological Role

House martins are insectivorous birds that help regulate flying insects. The report notes that one swallow may need around 150,000 flies to raise its brood.

Signal of Urban Degradation

Their decline is not only a biodiversity issue. It indicates that contemporary urban environments are removing basic nesting and feeding conditions.

Main Pressure Points

Pressure Typical Context Effect on Birds Effect on People
Smooth renovated facades Insulation upgrades and modern facade finishes Nests attach less easily and colonies fail to re-establish Late retrofits become harder and costlier
Loss of overhangs and irregularities Roof and facade simplification Less sheltered nesting space More conflict when birds try unsuitable points
Less available mud Impermeable surfaces and limited moist soil Nest building becomes harder; one nest may require 750-1,400 mud pellets Repeated failed nest attempts on occupied buildings
Insect decline Reduced urban habitat quality Lower food availability during breeding Weakens long-term colony stability in neighborhoods
Human conflict around droppings Entrances, balconies, and pedestrian routes Nests are blocked or removed when tolerance drops Complaints about hygiene, maintenance, and aesthetics

Ecological and Social Tension

  • Dropped tolerance from soiling is one of the main drivers of nest destruction.
  • Conservation succeeds only when hygiene and maintenance concerns are treated as core design issues.
  • The project goal is coexistence: protect nesting while making day-to-day building use realistic for residents.
