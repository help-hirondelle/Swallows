Guidance for House Owners

Owners, property managers, and residents are central to coexistence. The report shows that droppings beneath nests are one of the main reasons tolerance falls, so nuisance management must be planned together with nest protection.

Priority Actions

Do Not Remove Active Nests Casually

Nest destruction or blocking often happens when complaints rise, but this directly worsens population decline. Track nesting locations first and plan interventions through proper procedures.

Manage Droppings as a Core Task

Droppings boards (dropping shelves) can reduce soiling under nests, especially near entrances and walkways. They only work when cleaning and upkeep are planned.

Build Acceptance Through Communication

Explain why nests matter, what mitigation is in place, and who is responsible for maintenance. Early communication lowers complaints and reactive nest blocking.

Owner Checklist

Before renovation:
  • Document existing nests and repeat nesting points on facades.
  • Include nuisance management in project scope, not as a late add-on.
  • Check if facade changes will remove roughness, overhang, or sheltered points needed for nesting.
During operation:
  • Clean droppings boards regularly; unmanaged boards can accumulate waste and lose effectiveness.
  • Watch for side effects such as pigeons occupying boards or predator access if buildup is ignored.
  • Keep a simple log of complaints and interventions to target improvements.
