Guidance for City Planners and Municipalities

Municipalities are a key target audience in this project because they can regulate, fund, coordinate, and scale action. They are also well placed to reduce complaints, cleaning burden, and conflict while improving visible biodiversity outcomes.

Municipal Levers

Integrate Requirements in Planning

Include swallow-relevant criteria in renovation and construction procedures so solutions are preventive, not late corrections.

Fund and Coordinate Action

Support implementation across communes with shared technical guidance, pilot funding, and coordinated follow-up.

Map and Monitor Colonies

Build a colony map and track occupancy trends, conflict locations, and outcomes of installed measures.

Suggested Program Structure

Phase 1: Baseline mapping and coordination
  • Map colonies, complaint hotspots, and renovation zones likely to remove nesting options.
  • Align canton, municipality, and site-owner roles before implementation starts.
Phase 2: Pilot implementation
  • Deploy a package of measures (nests, droppings management, maintenance protocol, communication).
  • Include citizen participation in monitoring and maintenance where feasible.
Phase 3: Scale with metrics
  • Track complaint rate; cleaning interventions/hours/costs; number of installed solutions.
  • Track occupancy/usage rate and number of participating sites, then scale what performs.

Coordination Principle

Current measures often underperform because they are fragmented or implemented too late. Municipal coordination can convert isolated actions into a consistent program with lower conflict and stronger public trust.
